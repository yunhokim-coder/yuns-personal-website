Kwangwoon University | Master's Thesis Researcher

2022–2024

Designed and implemented a seven-session group coaching intervention, including participant recruitment, pilot testing, program refinement, and two-week follow-up assessment.

Structured the comparison condition to differ from the intervention in only two sessions, isolating the contribution of a targeted program component.

Applied a three-group quasi-experimental design (experimental, comparison, control; N = 24) and conducted reliability analyses, one-way ANOVA (baseline equivalence), paired and independent t tests, and mixed ANOVA using SPSS; grit showed a significant group-by-time improvement, while self-directed learning effects were not significant.

Pet Loss Syndrome Support Project | Researcher & Program Developer

2023–2025

Conducted a literature review and semi-structured interviews with individuals who had experienced and recovered from pet loss, followed by structured individual and focus group interviews to identify their support needs.

Designed and delivered a needs-based psychoeducational support program grounded in these findings, under the supervision of a Ph.D.-level clinical psychologist.

Kwangwoon University | Undergraduate Researcher

2019

Co-developed the Happiness Habit Scale (26-item pool; 163 young adults) using item, reliability, and exploratory factor analyses in SPSS, yielding a 15-item, three-factor measure (Agency, Positive Perception of Adversity, Interpersonal Relationships).

The initial 26-item scale showed Cronbach’s α = .808 and a significant positive association with the Satisfaction With Life Scale (r = .399, p < .001).

Kwangwoon University | Undergraduate Researcher

2019

Investigated whether perspective-taking and internal vs. external attribution increase multicultural acceptance (N = 108) using a 2 × 2 between-subjects, posttest-only control group experiment designed to reduce demand characteristics.

Analyzed data with two-way ANOVA in SPSS; neither manipulation had significant main or interaction effects, but gender differences were significant. As first author of the unpublished report, identified unclear stimulus materials and high data exclusion as limitations.
